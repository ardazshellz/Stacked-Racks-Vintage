# Stacked Racks Vintage Standing Instructions

These standing instructions apply to every future Listing Studio task in this repository.

## SIZING RULES

### Core principle

Flat **measurements** are the source of truth for the listed size. The tag is a **secondary** signal. Brand and era only tell you which way a tag is likely to mislead; never override the measurement with the tag. Always store and display **both inches and centimetres**.

**Primary measure:** pit-to-pit, with the garment laid flat and measured from armpit to armpit.

### Jeans and trousers

Do **not** use pit-to-pit. Use waist and inside leg.

| Measurement | How to measure and display |
| --- | --- |
| Waist | Measure flat across the top, then double it to get the full waist in inches—for example, 16 in flat = W32. Display in inches and centimetres. |
| Inside leg | Measure from the crotch seam to the hem. Display in inches and centimetres. |
| Leg opening | Measure and display for baggy and wide fits. Display in inches and centimetres. |

- List the size as `W[xx] L[xx]`.
- Verify the size by measuring even when the tag shows a W/L size; vintage denim waists often measure 1–2 in differently after wear or washing.
- Flag the listing when the measured waist differs from the tag by more than 1 in.
- State the cut: skinny, slim, straight, relaxed, baggy, wide or carpenter.
- Baggy and wide-leg cuts are selling points. Always state the leg opening or add: "baggy fit."

### Football shirts

Use pit-to-pit against the **men's T-shirts, polos and tops** chart, while applying these football-shirt-specific fit notes.

| Shirt type | Fit direction and required note |
| --- | --- |
| Modern or player-issue | Runs small and fitted. Add: "modern fitted, sizes small." |
| Retro or 90s replica | Often runs large and boxy. Add: "90s boxy fit." |
| European club shirts, especially Italian and Spanish | Runs especially small. |

- Always add a fit note.
- State whether the shirt is player-issue or replica.
- State whether it is a home or away shirt.
- Add the season or year when the sponsor or badge identifies it; this information drives value.

### Brand and era fit direction

Measurements decide the size. Use these tendencies **only** to identify how a tag may mislead and what fit note to add.

- **Runs large, long or boxy:** 90s–00s Nike, Adidas, Reebok, Champion, Carhartt, single-stitch US tees, and oversized 90s cuts. Add the note: "boxy / longline vintage fit."
- **Runs small, slim or fitted:** Italian and European brands—including GAS, Diesel, Stone Island, SSC Napoli and European football tops—Y2K slim cuts, and most women's vintage. Add the note: "fits slim / smaller than tag."
- **Unknown tendency:** do not assume a direction. Use measurements alone and add: "please check measurements."

### Mandatory rules

1. Set the listed size from **pit-to-pit** using the appropriate chart, not the tag.
2. If pit-to-pit falls between two sizes, round **down** unless the length clearly indicates the larger size; add a fit note.
3. If the tag disagrees with the measured size, trust the measurement and add: "Tag reads [X], measures like a modern [Y]."
4. **Never silently change a size the user has manually entered. Warn the user instead.**
5. If measurements are missing, do not guess a size from the tag alone. Flag that measurements are required before publishing.

### Always confirm and display on every listing

- Pit-to-pit in both inches and centimetres.
- Length, shoulder-to-hem, in both inches and centimetres. This catches items that fit long, such as boxy 90s Nike garments, when pit-to-pit alone does not.
- Cut or shape when notable: boxy, fitted, cropped, longline or oversized.
- A fit note whenever the measured size differs from the tag or the cut is unusual.

### Additional checks

6. **Unit sanity check:** pit-to-pit for adult tops is realistically about 17–30 in / 43–76 cm. If a value is far outside this range—for example, `62` with no unit—treat it as a likely unit mix-up (centimetres entered as inches or vice versa) and flag it instead of sizing from it. The current studio shows a `62 cm` sleeve placeholder; centimetres and inches must never be silently swapped.
7. **Modern versus vintage sizing:** state sizes as they fit today—for example, "fits like a modern M"—because a garment's original tag size is often not its modern equivalent.
8. **International size conversion:** when a tag uses a non-UK system (such as EU 46/48/50, Italian sizing, US S/M/L, or numeric collar/waist sizing), convert it to UK sizing and note the original. Measurements still decide the final listed size.
9. **Kids and youth:** if a tag shows age or youth sizing—for example, "L (14–16y)"—do not list it as an adult size. Flag it as youth and note the measurements.
10. **Shrinkage and stretch:** for heavily worn or thin vintage cotton, note that the garment may have shrunk or stretched and that measurements are the reliable guide.
11. **Gender of cut:** judge men's versus women's from cut and measurements, not only the department field. A tapered waist, shorter length and narrow shoulders may indicate a women's or unisex-small fit even when an item is tagged as men's, such as the Napoli polo. Flag any conflict with the entered department.
12. **Consistency:** the size field, measurements and fit note must not contradict one another. If they do, stop and warn the user.
